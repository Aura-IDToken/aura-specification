## 1. What was done

BC-02.1 v1 carried one digest field, `received.recomputed_sha256`. BC-02 §14
states B-VAL-014 over two operands, `receipt_digest` and `input_segment_sha256`.
Under v1 the only way to evaluate that condition was to read one field twice,
making the equality a tautology of the receipt's shape.

`B-VAL-014-DEFINITION-RECONCILIATION-RECORD.md` §16.1 recorded this as PRE-01,
a material unmet execution precondition. This record closes it **at the schema
layer only**, by the minimal correction that makes the two operands
independently observable and provenance-identifiable.

The meaning of B-VAL-014 is unchanged. Whether the two operand values are equal
remains an unexecuted observation.

### C.4 `fixture_artifact_identity` — semantic mismatch with executed v1 receipts

v1 §4 required the field but never stated that its value is a digest. Both
receipts produced by the Controlled P-01 Handoff carry:

```text
fixture_artifact_identity = "FIX-DIGEST-P01.canonical.json"
```

which is an artifact **name** occupying the identity field. Under the PRE-01
instruction §5 and v2 §4:

```text
fixture_artifact_name     = FIX-DIGEST-P01.canonical.json      (label)
fixture_artifact_identity = SHA-256(exact issued artifact octets)
                          = ecf9e98ec0641e23113ff3ce8bdc78d0ddd249886517fd4a7f68cc83d4e65667
```

v2 rejects a `fixture_artifact_identity` that is not a SHA-256 hexadecimal
digest, so a name can no longer occupy the identity field.

**The mismatch is reported, not reconciled.** The two executed v1 receipts are
not rewritten, are not reinterpreted as if they had carried a digest, and no
retroactive binding is declared. Correcting the recorded evidence would require
a controlled re-handoff, which this task is not authorized to perform.

### C.5 Schema deficiency identified — unresolved-value representation

v1 §10 required `missing value ≠ UNKNOWN ≠ ABSENT` while prohibiting `null` and
providing **no representation for any of those states**. A v1 receipt could not
record an unbound field at all.

This is material in v2 because `issuance_id` is required and no issuance record
exists. v2 §10 adds:

```text
UnresolvedValue { state ∈ {UNKNOWN, ABSENT, ERROR, REJECT}, detail }
```

transported as a JSON object, distinguishable by type from a bound string value.
It is an engineering representation of an unbound field value. It introduces no
protocol state and no normative requirement, and in v2 it may be used **only**
for `fixture.issuance_id`.

### D.3 Raw material is recorded once, referenced by the operand

Each operand **names** its material through `source_material_ref` and never
carries a second copy of it. Both `received.raw_octets` and
`input_segment.octets` keep octet semantics in the logical model; Base64 appears
only at the JSON transport boundary, and each digest is taken over the decoded
octets, never over the Base64 characters.

### PRE-01-004 — PASS

Four independent checks:

1. **Differential falsification.** With `raw_input_encoding = base16`,

   equal**. Two values read from one buffer would necessarily be equal here.
   Each reproduces SHA-256 of its own material.
2. **Identity case.** With `raw_input_encoding = identity` the two values

3. **Structural.** The two operand observers are distinct callables with
   different parameter sets, and neither admits a parameter matching

4. **Recorded source.** `COPIED_FROM_OTHER_OPERAND` is representable in the

### PRE-01-006 — PASS

`received.octet_length == len(received.raw_octets)`. In the differential case it

### PRE-01-007 — PASS

## F. Confirmation — no assignment, no alias

```text
input_segment_sha256   ≠   receipt_digest      as a programmatic
                                               assignment / alias relationship
```

Confirmed. In the reference implementation:

- the two operands are produced at two call sites by two distinct private
  observers, `_observe_input_segment()` and `_observe_received_buffer()`;
- each observer receives **only its own** raw material and can reach neither the
  other's buffer nor the other's value;
- no assignment, copy, or alias exists between the two operand fields, and none
  is reachable from any public entry point;
- `receive()` exposes no digest parameter, so neither operand can be supplied
  from outside;
- the two source buffers are distinct objects, enforced at construction;
- v2 §6.1 (R-INV-05) prohibits the assignment at schema level, and
  `COPIED_FROM_OTHER_OPERAND` makes a violation nameable in the record rather
  than invisible.

**The two VALUES may be equal in a later controlled execution.** For P-01 under
`raw_input_encoding = identity` they are expected to coincide. That equality
would be an **observed result of two independent calculations**, never an
assignment — and observing it is B-VAL-014's job, not this record's.
